JODE Launcher

Privacy Policy

Effective 1 September 2026 · Last updated 1 September 2026

JODE Launcher does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no advertising, and no network access of any kind. Everything the app reads stays on your device.

In one line: the app has no internet permission, so nothing it touches can leave your phone — not to us, and not to anyone else.

1. Who this policy is from

This policy applies to the Android application JODE Launcher (package com.zypido.jode), published by Joydeep Setua (“we”, “us”). You can reach us at zypido@gmail.com.

2. What the app is

JODE Launcher is a minimal Android home screen. It shows a clock, your screen time for today, your five most recently opened apps, and a search field for launching any installed app by name. It has no widgets, folders, news feed, weather, contacts, web search, or account system.

3. Data we collect

None. We do not collect, receive, store, process, or have access to any personal or device information about you. We operate no servers and no backend. We cannot identify you, and we have no way of knowing that you have installed the app at all.

The app contains no third-party SDKs, no analytics or measurement libraries, no advertising networks, no crash-reporting service, and no tracking of any kind.

4. Information the app reads on your device

To function as a home screen, the app asks Android for certain information. All of it is read on the device, used immediately to draw the screen, and never transmitted anywhere.

What is read Why Where it goes
The list of apps installed on your device that have a launcher icon — their names, icons, and package identifiers To display and search your app list, and to launch the app you choose Held in memory while the app runs; never written off-device
Android usage statistics — which apps were opened and for how long To order the five recent apps and total today’s screen time Read from the system on demand; never written off-device
A device-administrator entry limited to the single “lock screen” policy So double-tapping the home screen can lock the device, if you enable it Held by Android; grants no access to any content on your device

The app requests neither location, camera, microphone, contacts, calendar, SMS, call logs, files, photos, nor any other runtime permission. It does not read your notifications, messages, browsing history, clipboard, or the contents of any other app. It does not use Android’s QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission; the app list comes from a narrow declaration that reveals only the apps a home screen must be able to show.

5. Information stored on your device

The app keeps a small amount of state in its own private storage area, readable only by the app itself:

  • A flag recording that the first-run welcome screen has been shown.
  • A short local list of apps you launched from this launcher, used to order the recents row if you have not granted usage access.
  • A single number: the highest daily screen-time total seen so far, so today can be compared against it.

This is stored using Android’s private per-app storage. It is not readable by other apps, it is not included in Android cloud backups (the app disables backup), and it is deleted entirely when you uninstall the app or clear its data from Android Settings.

6. Permissions, and how to turn them off

Usage access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS)

Optional. This is special access, not a runtime permission: declaring it grants nothing and shows no prompt, and it takes effect only if you switch it on yourself under Settings → Apps → Special app access → Usage access. It lets the launcher show apps you opened anywhere on the device, and today’s screen time. Without it the launcher still works, falling back to the launches it performed itself and hiding the screen-time figure. You can revoke it at any time in the same Settings screen.

Device administrator (lock screen)

Optional. If you enable it, the app holds one device-administrator policy — force lock — which allows the double-tap-to-lock gesture to turn off the screen. It does not allow the app to wipe your device, read or change your password, monitor your activity, or access any content. You can revoke it at any time under Settings → Security → Device admin apps.

Home app role

Optional. Setting JODE Launcher as your default home app is entirely your choice and can be changed at any time under Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app.

10. Security

The app transmits nothing, so there is no transmission to intercept and no server to breach. On-device state is confined to Android’s private per-app storage and is protected by the operating system’s own sandboxing.

We hold no personal data about any user, so there is nothing for us to produce, correct, or erase. You retain full control over the on-device state described above through Android’s own settings.
